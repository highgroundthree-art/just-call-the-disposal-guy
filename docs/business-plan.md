## Executive summary

Just Call the Disposal Guy requests a $16,000 loan, alongside $4,000 of owner cash, to launch a flat-price garbage disposal replacement service in the Phoenix metro. The service does one job only: it swaps a dead plug-in disposal for one of two new models, at $589 or $599 installed, with no upselling.

The business runs on a platform model like a ride-share. The customer books and pays online. The system screens the job by photo, ships the disposal to the customer's home, and schedules the install. An installer arrives with tools only. Every job splits the same way: $200 to the installer, about $100 to the company, and the rest covers the unit, taxes, card fees and advertising.

The owner, a licensed Arizona contractor, is the only installer in Year 1 at about 5 jobs a week. Contract installers join in Year 2. By Year 3 contractors do every install and the owner runs the platform.

## The business and the problem

A homeowner with a dead disposal has two poor choices today: install one themselves, or call a plumber and get an open-ended quote plus a pitch for other work. Just Call the Disposal Guy offers a third: one price, shown before booking, for one job.

- **What we sell:** replacement of an existing plug-in garbage disposal, including the new unit, installation, haul-away of the old unit and a 1-year leak-free install guarantee.
- **What we never do:** diagnostics, repairs, hardwired units, or add-on sales. Jobs outside the standard are declined at the photo check, before any money is spent.
- **Why it can be cheap to run:** two models only, both shipped with a power cord. Every job uses the same parts, the same steps and about an hour on site.
- **Legal form:** a single-member Arizona LLC, to be formed. The owner holds an Arizona contractor's license.

## Service, pricing and money per job

The price is built backward from a fixed split: $200 to the installer, $100 to the company, and everything else at cost. Offering only two models removes the one large variable, the price of the disposal.

| --- | --- | --- |
| **Customer price** | **$589** | **$599** |
| Disposal unit (estimate) | −$170 | −$180 |
| Sales tax on unit (~8.5%) | −$14 | −$15 |
| Card fees (~3.4%) | −$20 | −$21 |
| Supplies | −$10 | −$10 |
| Advertising per booked job | −$60 | −$60 |
| Installer payout | −$200 | −$200 |
| **Left for the company** | **$115** | **$113** |
| Fixed overhead share (insurance, software, bookkeeping) | varies with volume | varies with volume |

After fixed overhead, the company keeps about $88 a job in Year 1 and about $98–$99 in Years 2 and 3.

Both units are 3/4 horsepower. Arizona treats disposal replacement as repair-and-replace work, so the customer's invoice carries no sales tax line; the company pays tax when it buys the unit ([Arizona DOR](https://azdor.gov/transaction-privilege-tax/contracting-guidelines/contracting-faqs)). A CPA should confirm this before launch.

Payment: $100 deposit by card at booking; the balance is charged to the same card when the installer marks the job done. The customer signs the terms and the card authorization online.

## Market and competition

Maricopa County replaces roughly 80,000 disposals a year by our estimate; the Year 3 plan of 1,660 installs is about 2% of that.

**How the estimate is built**

- 1,913,901 housing units in Maricopa County as of July 2023 ([U.S. Census](https://census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/maricopacountyarizona,US/PST045221)).
- About half of U.S. homes have a disposal ([US Inspect](https://www.usinspect.com/?p=5064)). Phoenix-area housing is newer than average, so the local share may be higher.
- A disposal lasts about 12 years on average ([Bob Vila, citing the home inspectors' association](https://bobvila.com/articles/how-long-do-garbage-disposals-last/)).
- 1.9 million homes × 50% ÷ 12 years ≈ 80,000 replacements a year.

**Competition and price position**

| Alternative | Typical cost | Our difference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plumber or handyman | $400–$500 for unit and install, nationally ([This Old House](https://www.thisoldhouse.com/kitchens/garbage-disposal-installation-cost)); $150–$900 full range ([Angi](https://angi.com/articles/garbage-disposal-last.htm)) | Price known before booking; no service-call fee; no upsell |
| Do it yourself | Cost of the unit | No tools, no trip to the store, old unit hauled away |
| Big-box store install programs | Varies | One company from booking to guarantee |

Our $589 and $599 sit above the typical national figure. That is the plan's biggest open question: the price buys certainty and convenience, and whether Phoenix homeowners will pay the premium is unproven until the first jobs are sold. The fallback is a lower price funded by cheaper units; see the sensitivity table.

## Marketing and sales

Customers find us at the moment the disposal dies, so the budget goes to search first. The plan allows $60 of advertising per booked job.

| Channel | Role | Benchmark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Google Local Services Ads | Main paid channel; pay per lead, top of the results page | Plumbing average: $57 a lead, 44.5% of leads book ([Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/plumbing-google-ads-statistics)) |
| Google Business Profile and reviews | Free map listing; every customer gets a review request by text | No cost |
| Google Search ads on exact disposal phrases | Fills gaps Local Services Ads miss | Plumbing average: $10.49 a click ([Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/sub-services/plumbing-ppc)) |
| Nextdoor, Facebook, Instagram | Short swap videos; neighborhood word of mouth | Low cost, slower |

The plumbing benchmarks work out to about $128 of ads per booked job, more than twice our $60 allowance. We expect to beat the benchmark for three reasons: the website shows the full price, so price shoppers screen themselves out; booking happens online without a sales call; and reviews and referrals cost nothing as they build. The sensitivity table shows the result if we do not.

The sale itself needs no salesperson. The site shows two options and one price each, and the customer books, uploads photos and pays the deposit in one visit.

## Operations

The system handles every routine step; a person is needed only for the install itself and for exceptions. The detailed process is in the companion [Operations Structure](https://claude.ai/artifact/RfeYoqUXY546bExmkJymDt).

1. **Book.** The customer picks a model, uploads five under-sink photos, signs the terms and pays the $100 deposit.
2. **Screen.** The photos are checked against a fixed list before any money is spent on a unit. Retakes are requested by text. Hardwired or non-standard jobs are declined and refunded.
3. **Order.** The disposal ships from Amazon Business to the customer's address, paid with a spending-capped card used for nothing else.
4. **Schedule.** Once the unit is delivered, the customer picks an install time in the following week. Everyone with a delivered unit is on a move-up list, so cancellations are refilled by whoever has waited longest.
5. **Install.** The installer arrives with tools, swaps the unit, tests for leaks, photographs the finished work and hauls away the old unit.
6. **Collect and pay.** The balance is charged to the card on file. The installer payout and the bookkeeping entry follow automatically.

The company holds almost no inventory, needs no shop or warehouse, and collects the deposit before it buys the unit.

**The request:** $16,000, repaid over 5 years. At an assumed 10% rate the payment is about $340 a month.

**Best-fit program:** the SBA Microloan. It lends up to $50,000, must be repaid within six years, and typically carries 8% to 13% interest ([SBA](https://www.sba.gov/es/node/8668), [Merchant Maverick](https://www.merchantmaverick.com/?p=49238)). The average microloan in fiscal 2025 was $16,131 ([Nav](https://www.nav.com/business-financing-options/sba-microloans)), so this request is ordinary for the program. Microloans come through local nonprofit lenders, and SCORE can point to the ones serving Phoenix.

**Repayment sources:** company operating profit first; in Year 1 the owner's $200 installer pay on every job is a second cushion; the owner's teaching salary stands behind a personal guarantee.

**Assumptions**

- Year 1 ramps from 2 installs a week to 5 over the first three months, then holds at 5.

- In Year 1 the installer payouts of $46,000 go to the owner for his own labor, on top of company profit.
- Years 2 and 3 depend on demand that Year 1 must prove. A lender should weigh Year 1 most heavily.
